Part 1 · How they did it

Four eras, and none of them was just a Python script.

Each leap changed who did the heavy lifting: first the math, then the engineers, then neural networks, and now LLM-style models.

users × movies
~2000 – 2010

Collaborative filtering

“People who liked X also liked Y.” Amazon published its item-to-item method in 2003.

In the Netflix Prize (2006–2009), Netflix offered a million dollars to whoever improved its recommender by 10%. Matrix factorization won: it fills the gaps in the ratings table with “hidden tastes.” Those are the ancestors of today’s embeddings.

~2010 – 2016

Linear models and hand-crafted features

For ads, Google and Facebook used logistic regression with billions of variables, plus gradient-boosted trees. Facebook published a 2014 paper combining the two.

The heavy lifting was done by engineers inventing features: “how many times did this user like this page in the last 7 days?”, “time of day × content type.” Entire teams did nothing else.

~2016 – 2022

Deep learning and the funnel

YouTube published its neural-network recommender in 2016, Google released Wide & Deep that same year, Meta published DLRM in 2019 and Alibaba published DIN. That’s where the funnel almost everyone still uses comes from.

Networks learn on their own what used to be designed by hand. TikTok added online training (the Monolith paper, 2022): the model updates every few minutes.

ModelsTwo-tower, multi-task
InfraTerabyte embeddings, GPUs

2024 – today

Generative recommenders

Your history is treated like a sentence, and the model predicts your next action the way an LLM predicts the next word. Meta showed these models follow scaling laws: more compute brings predictable gains.

Some companies are already trying to tear down the 2016 funnel and replace it with a single model. More on that in part 2.

What’s underneath

Infrastructure is half the secret.

The model is just one piece. The hard part is feeding it fresh data, serving it to billions of people and measuring every change.

Memory

Terabyte-sized embedding tables

Every user, video, hashtag and advertiser has its own vector. Those tables don’t fit on a single machine, so they are spread across entire clusters. In many recommenders they outweigh the neural network itself.

Real time

Feature stores and streaming

With Kafka and Flink, what you did three seconds ago already counts for what you see next.

Learning

Online training

Models that update every few minutes instead of once a day.

Measurement

A/B testing at scale

Thousands of experiments at once. No change ships without being tested against real metrics.

Hardware

GPUs and in-house chips

Meta designs its own chips (MTIA), and Google has TPUs.

        Part 2 · The cutting edge, 2024 – 2026

        What the best teams are doing right now.

        LLMs are coming in hard, but in a far more interesting way than putting ChatGPT inside the app.

        The biggest shift

        Your history as a language

        Instead of asking “what are the odds of a like on this video?”, the model reads your sequence of actions the way an LLM reads a sentence, and predicts the next one.

        Beyond finance

        Three places with a real opportunity.

        Not all with the same certainty. They go from the strongest bet to the shakiest.

        agent ↔ agent
        02Main bet

        Payments and identity for AI agents

        If agents are going to hire services, pay for APIs and negotiate with each other, they need three things blockchain already solves:

        Programmable moneyNo bank account required.

        Cryptographic identityNo paperwork designed for humans.

        Rules that enforce themselvesNo referee in the middle.

        The traditional system has no obvious alternative: it was built for humans with documents.

        Here blockchain doesn’t compete with AI. It serves it.

        person · date · origin
        03Real problem, unclear winner

        Proof of personhood and origin

        When any content can be synthetic, proving that a human made something, or that a document existed on a given date, becomes valuable.

        Did a human make it? Did it exist on that date? Is it the original?

        It’s not clear blockchain wins here. Cryptography alone can solve much of it without a public chain. What is certain: the problem is real and growing.
